## Overview

Craft Crew was building four products at once, with no shared technical direction across any of them:

- A **radiology platform** handling real medical images for Egyptian imaging centres, with an AI stage ahead
- **Operational systems** for those same imaging centres
- An **education and courses platform**
- A **carbon-accounting platform** for a client in Morocco

Each had been started differently. With a small team spread across all four, every move between projects cost days of relearning.

## My role

Senior front-end engineer, owning every front-end decision across the four products, and working on the backend in Nest.js.

## Approach

- Chose Next.js and TypeScript on the front end and Nest.js on the back for one reason: a single language across the system lets a small team move between products without changing how it thinks
- Put the standards into the tooling rather than into documents nobody reads — commit-stage checks through Husky, so a rule lives in the commit process instead of on a wiki page
- Settled on one shared approach to forms and validation, so every product behaves the same way and a developer arriving from another product already knows how it works
- Took on the intern team: reviewing their code, allocating their work, and explaining why something breaks rather than quietly correcting it
- Building an internal tooling set that turns a Figma link into front-end code, so repeated design translation across four products becomes review rather than retyping. Still in trial, not yet team-adopted

## Where it stands

Two of the four products have been delivered. The stack chosen in January is still the company standard, and the interns have moved on to larger tasks than they could handle when they started.
